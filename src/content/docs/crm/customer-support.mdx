## Everything Support, In One Screen <Badge text="CRM Feature" variant="success" size="small" />

The **Customer Support** portal replaces the old standalone Tickets page. Instead of jumping between an escalation queue, a ticket list and a conversation view, everything a support agent needs sits behind one left-hand menu — with a live chat console that adapts to whichever channel the customer is actually on.

Find it under **CRM → Customer Support**.

## Live Chat

### The queue

Conversations arrive here in two states:

- **Waiting** — the bot escalated and nobody has claimed it yet
- **Active** — an agent has taken over and is replying

Both lists update in real time. You do not need to refresh, and you should not have to wait for a poll: claiming, resolving and new messages all push to every agent's screen as they happen.

### The chat looks like the customer's channel

The console re-skins itself to match wherever the customer actually is — WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, Messenger, Discord or the web widget. It is a small thing that prevents a real mistake: replying with the tone or formatting of the wrong platform.

<Aside type="tip" title="Full context before you type">
Above the live thread you'll find the **bot conversation that came before you** — collapsed by default. Open it to see exactly what the customer already asked and what the AI already told them, so you don't repeat a question they've answered twice.
</Aside>

### Taking over

<Steps>
1. Open a waiting conversation and press **Accept**.
2. **The bot goes quiet immediately.** It will not reply again on this conversation while you hold it.
3. Reply normally. Your messages are delivered on the customer's own channel — they never see a channel switch or a new number.
4. When you're done, press **Resolve**.
</Steps>

On resolve, the bot resumes and the conversation you just had is **written into its memory**, with each turn attributed: the customer, the AI, and you by name. The next time that customer writes in, the bot knows what was agreed and who agreed it.

<Aside type="caution" title="Your name, not your username">
Customers see your **display name**. If you haven't set one, they see your login username — which for an account literally named `admin` reads badly. Set it in **Settings → Profile → Display name** (e.g. *Ana from Support*).
</Aside>

### Sending files, photos, audio and video

The composer accepts every format the platform can deliver:

| Type | Formats |
|---|---|
| Images | JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP |
| Audio | MP3, OGG/Opus, WAV, M4A, AAC |
| Video | MP4, MOV, M4V, 3GP, MKV, WebM |
| Documents | PDF, Word, Excel, CSV, TXT |

You can also **record a voice note** directly in the console — press the microphone, speak, send. It arrives as a proper push-to-talk bubble, not a file attachment.

Documents arrive for the customer as a tappable card showing the file type and size, rather than a bare link they might not notice.

<Aside type="note" title="Oversized video is handled for you">
WhatsApp will not accept an arbitrarily large video. If yours is over the limit it is automatically compressed to fit; if it still cannot be made small enough it is sent as a document instead, so the customer always receives the file one way or another.
</Aside>

### Internal notes

Below the conversation there's a collapsed **Internal notes** section. Notes are visible to your team only and are deliberately kept **out of the message thread**, so there is never a moment where you have to work out whether a line was actually delivered to the customer.

### Acting on the CRM without leaving the chat

The **CRM panel** button opens a side drawer where you can, mid-conversation:

- Create a **lead** from a first-time contact
- Attach a **contact** to the ticket
- Move an open **deal** to its next pipeline stage
- Create a **new deal**

Your bots can do the same things autonomously — capture a lead, progress a deal, record a note — so a conversation that starts with the AI and finishes with you produces one continuous CRM history rather than two disconnected halves.

Live Chat shows you what has been escalated. **Active Conversations** shows you everything else: every conversation your bots are handling right now, with the channel, the customer, how long it has been running and the last thing said.

From here you can:

- Open a **transcript** of any ongoing bot conversation
- **Take over** any of them with one click — no escalation required

That second point is the useful one. You do not have to wait for a customer to get frustrated enough to ask for a human, or for the bot to decide it is stuck. If you can see a conversation going sideways, you can step in.

<Aside type="caution" title="Two channels can't be taken over">
Conversations arriving through the **REST API** or **Unipile** are shown for visibility but cannot be taken over, because there is no live channel to bridge your replies back through.
</Aside>

## Escalation

### How a conversation reaches a human

Three routes, and they run independently:

<Steps>
1. **The customer asks.** "Let me talk to a person." The AI recognises the intent and escalates.
2. **The bot gives up.** After a configurable number of unsuccessful attempts on the same problem, it stops trying and fetches someone.
3. **Frustration is detected.** Optionally, the AI escalates when a customer's tone turns clearly negative.
</Steps>

### Where it goes

If an agent is **online**, the conversation lands in the Live Chat queue and everyone sees it immediately.

If **nobody is available**, a ticket is created instead so the request is not lost, and the customer is told a person will follow up.

### If nobody claims it in time

An escalation that sits unclaimed does not simply expire. The bot returns to the conversation, offers the customer a **contact-back**, and opens a ticket containing a summary of what happened — so the follow-up starts with context rather than "sorry, can you explain again?"

### Configuring it

Escalation behaviour is set **per bot**, under **Assistants → your bot → Handoff**:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Default assignee** | Who new escalations are routed to |
| **Human request threshold** | How many failed attempts before the bot escalates on its own |
| **Auto-escalate on frustration** | Escalate when the customer's tone turns negative |
| **Escalation keywords** | Phrases that trigger an immediate handoff |

The portal's **Escalation Settings** section links straight to these.

## When an agent replies from their own phone

If your team answers a WhatsApp customer directly from the WhatsApp app rather than the console, that reply is **captured back into the ticket** automatically. The transcript stays complete, and the bot's memory stays accurate, even when someone works outside the portal.

The Analytics section reports on the support operation itself:

- **Open handoffs** and how many arrived **today**
- **Time to claim** and **time to resolve** (median and 95th percentile)
- **Abandon rate** — escalations nobody picked up
- **Critical** — conversations breaching your response window
- **Online agents** right now
- Breakdown **by channel** and **per agent**

Percentiles matter more than averages here: a p95 time-to-claim tells you what your slowest customers experienced, which an average will happily hide.
